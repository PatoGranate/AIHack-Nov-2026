BOT COMPARISON EXPERIMENT — 14 November 2026

Roster: v1 archive plus submissions A-F, each frozen with a SHA-256 hash in
Tournament/manifest.json. Current kit/bot.py matches C byte-for-byte, so it is
not counted twice. Competition datasets labelled v1-v4 are distinct from these
source archive names; no unverified mapping to v4 is assumed.

Main experiment: 56 rounds of 1,000 seven-player hands, with all seven bots at
one table, stacks reset to 2,000, blinds 10/20. For each of eight independent
deck seeds, rotate all seven bots through all seven seats. Each bot plays
56,000 hands, with 392,000 player-hand observations in total. This is a league
comparison in this particular field, not isolated heads-up matchups.

Fast mode uses LocalPlayer: actions are recorded but external per-decision
timeouts are not enforced. Bots retain their own original compute budgeting.
Two worker processes are used. Bot RNGs are seeded, but time-limited simulation
counts still depend on machine scheduling. The final confidence intervals use
eight independent deck-seed blocks, each averaging its seven seat rotations;
they do not pretend the rotations or individual hands are independent.

Runtime screen: seven additional 1,000-hand rounds under SubprocessPlayer's
10 ms decisions and 10,000 ms time bank, one rotation of the seven seats.
No confidence interval is claimed from that single deck-seed block. Windows
subprocess scheduling differs from the hosted tournament environment. This
screen measures local timing reliability rather than certifying deployment.
The first timing-screen round overlapped the tail of the main league; later
rounds ran after those worker processes finished. Background machine load is
not controlled. Treat timeout counts as diagnostic, not a pure bot-speed ranking.

Understanding decisions: reuse several actual postflop states from round zero
and ask every bot to decide on each identical state. These probes use fresh
opponent priors, not the learned statistics from the originating hand. Capture
the bot's computed equity where exposed by its equity/simulate routine, its
chosen action and simulation count where available. Those are numerical model
estimates, not natural-language thoughts or ground-truth equity. Range-based
and random-opponent equities use different assumptions. Probe samples can vary
with runtime; decisions do not by themselves prove one bot is correct.

Playing style: voluntary preflop participation (VPIP) and preflop raising (PFR)
are counted once per hand. Postflop aggression is raises / all postflop
decisions, including checks and folds in its denominator. These are observed
styles in this league, not opening-chart cutoffs or standard aggression factor.

Files remain under ExternalAnalysis. Playing sources and uploaded bots are
unchanged. Raw round records and frozen snapshots allow independent inspection.
