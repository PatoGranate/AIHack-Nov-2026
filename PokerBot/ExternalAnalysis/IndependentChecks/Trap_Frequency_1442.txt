V7 TRAP OPPORTUNITY FREQUENCY CHECK

Single process, two sequential seven-player tables, seeds 717171 and 717172,
1000 hands each. Hero seats 2 and 3. Field: two maniacs, two LAGs, a station,
and a TAG. Agents' RNGs seeded; original v7 computation budget retained.
No playing source modified. Frozen source: trap_v7_snapshot.py; SHA256
b8a22e4b9085cf8c634a129deba71faf479834cac7ab127b84f551e2b0f0c16a.

Seed 717171: 242 hero postflop decisions, 1 trap trigger, 0 questioned subset.
Seed 717172: 175 hero postflop decisions, 3 trap triggers, 0 questioned subset.
Combined: 417 postflop decisions, 4 trap triggers, 0 cases where every qualifying
aggressive opponent had already acted and none qualified remaining after hero.
All 14 seat-round adapters reported zero errors. Chip conservation passed both
tables. The simulation itself took approximately 1.75 seconds across both tables,
excluding import/prebuild/setup. Full adapter stats and trigger records are in
trap_frequency_results.json.

Since the questioned subset was absent, no model opportunity-cost figure exists
for it. This does not refute the reachable branch case proved separately, but
provides no evidence that it is frequent enough to justify a last-minute change.
Recommend keeping the frozen v7 gate intact and testing this refinement later.
Do not extrapolate a field-wide prevalence or an EV bound from this small sample.

Model gaps are recorded for ordinary triggers as best model bet EV minus model
check baseline, both raw and with the configured aggression bonus. They are
biased model diagnostics, not realised loss: checking induces future actions
that the static check baseline does not model. No counterfactual A/B loss was
measured. LocalPlayer external deadlines are unenforced and wall-clock sample
counts vary with host load. The positive hero chip results are incidental and
not used as evidence about the trap variant.
