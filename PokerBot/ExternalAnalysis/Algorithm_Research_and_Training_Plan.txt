ONLINE ALGORITHM RESEARCH AND TRAINING RECOMMENDATIONS
External analyst — 14 November 2026

RECOMMENDATION TO THE TEAM

Build an offline external-sampling MCCFR blueprint, initially for a deliberately
small poker abstraction, and export a compact mixed policy into bot.py. Keep
the existing bot as a benchmark and fallback. Add population-based training and
conditional opponent exploitation after validating the core learner. Consider
Deep CFR if the tabular abstraction becomes the limiting factor. Defer live
subgame solving until measured runtime proves it fits.

This is my recommendation for THIS project, inferred from published methods and
the inspected code. It is not a claim that one algorithm is universally best,
or that an implementation of it automatically beats v1-v4. Offline training may
use richer tooling; the uploaded bot must still satisfy the supplied kit's
single-file/standard-library, 512 MB, 10 ms/decision and 10 s/round requirements.
Do not assume a research system's live search fits those constraints.

1. PLURIBUS: MOST RELEVANT MULTIPLAYER RESEARCH REFERENCE

Brown and Sandholm's Pluribus demonstrated strong performance against elite
humans in six-player no-limit hold'em. It combines an offline self-play blueprint
with depth-limited search. Its multiplayer results are empirical; the familiar
two-player zero-sum convergence guarantees do not simply transfer to multiplayer.
Sources: https://noambrown.com/papers/19-Science-Superhuman.pdf
https://noambrown.com/papers/19-Science-Superhuman_Supp.pdf

Project adaptation (my proposal): reproduce the architecture in stages, starting
with the blueprint only. Train for the actual seven/eight-seat reset-stack game,
100 BB stacks, fixed blinds and chip payoff. Do not import six-max opening
frequencies as if positions and opponent counts were interchangeable. At runtime
sample actions from exported frequencies rather than choose the same threshold
action every time. Benchmark blueprint-only play before adding search.

2. EXTERNAL-SAMPLING MONTE CARLO CFR: FIRST TRAINING IMPLEMENTATION

MCCFR samples portions of the extensive-form game instead of traversing the full
tree. External sampling samples chance and opponents' actions while considering
the updating player's available actions. Counterfactual regret updates compare
alternative actions at information sets. Accumulated average strategy is a key
output; the final iteration's strategy is not a substitute for it.
Source: Lanctot et al., Monte Carlo Sampling for Regret Minimization in Extensive
Games: https://www.mlanctot.info/files/papers/nips09mccfr.pdf

Project implementation proposal:
- Start with Kuhn/Leduc poker to validate regret updates, sampling probabilities,
  reach weighting and average-policy accumulation against a reference solver.
- Then create a small no-limit abstraction. Information-set features must use
  hero cards, public board/actions, position, remaining stacks, pot, player count
  and betting history. Exclude opponents' actual hidden cards from policy inputs.
- Training traversal may access simulator state to compute counterfactual returns;
  that does not permit the acting policy to condition on hidden information.
- Use a small legal action menu: fold/check/call, selected pot fractions and
  all-in. Include minimum legal raises where needed; deduplicate after clamping.
- Preserve earlier private/public observations when defining information sets;
  casually merging histories can introduce imperfect recall and change guarantees.
- Save strategy averages, visited-set counts, training seeds, model format and
  chip utility definitions with checkpoints.
- Export action probabilities to an embedded compressed table or compact model.
  Measure memory, load time and latency before increasing abstraction size.

Correct the existing engine-facing EV/accounting issues first. A trainer that
misprices callers, side pots or folded hands can learn the wrong game faster.
The current Monte Carlo equity estimator is not MCCFR: simulating showdown
equity does not itself train a strategically balanced policy.

3. DCFR / CFR+: COMPARE UPDATE RULES UNDER EQUAL COMPUTE

Discounted CFR reweights historical regret and strategy contributions; the
published work studies faster solving through these changes. CFR+ is another
important regret-update baseline. Algorithm details and averaging choices matter.
Source: Brown and Sandholm, Solving Imperfect-Information Games via Discounted
Regret Minimization: https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04040

Project adaptation: use plain/reference CFR to establish correctness on tiny
games, then compare an established DCFR or CFR+ implementation. Do not splice
full-tree CFR+ updates into a sampled trainer and assume all theoretical or
empirical properties survive. Compare convergence and held-out playing strength
per wall-clock training hour, not iteration count alone.

4. DEEP CFR AND SINGLE DEEP CFR: SCALE AFTER A TABULAR BASELINE

Deep CFR replaces large tabular representations with neural approximation of
counterfactual regret behaviour. It uses sampled training and learned networks
to generalise across states, reducing dependence on handcrafted card abstraction.
Source: Brown et al., Deep Counterfactual Regret Minimization:
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/brown19b.html

Single Deep CFR avoids training a separate average-strategy network and reports
reduced approximation error and improved results in the paper's poker settings.
Its representation of historical strategies has deployment implications.
Source: Steinberger, Single Deep Counterfactual Regret Minimization:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.07621

Project adaptation: train neural models offline, then distil to a small deployable
policy or table. A distilled policy is a new approximation and needs its own
strength test. Pure-Python inference must be timed; do not upload a PyTorch-based
agent into a standard-library-only runner. Use reservoir sampling and the
algorithm's correct iteration/sample weighting, not a generic recent-transition
buffer. Check that a proposed SD-CFR checkpoint ensemble fits memory before
choosing it over a single model. Neither cited result proves seven/eight-player
equilibrium convergence or superiority in this field.

5. POPULATION TRAINING / PSRO: IMPROVE AGAINST THE FIELD

Policy-space response oracle methods maintain policy populations and train
responses to mixtures. Generalised PSRO work considers many-player settings.
Source: A Generalized Training Approach for Multiagent Learning:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12823

Project adaptation: keep v1, v2, v3, v4, new blueprint checkpoints and diverse
opponent archetypes in a frozen league. Evaluate seat-balanced matchups and train
against a mixture, retaining older policies to detect cycles. Statistical clones
are uncertain approximations, so vary their parameters. Preserve a completely
held-out field. A practical league inspired by PSRO is not automatically a full
PSRO implementation; label it accurately unless response-oracle and mixture
updates are actually implemented.

For this team's score, field exploitation matters as well as robustness. Use a
blueprint as the default, then cautiously adjust against repeated conditional
fold/call/raise evidence. Tune the adjustment on held-out opponents. This is my
proposed hybrid; it has no claimed multiplayer safety guarantee.

6. REBEL / STUDENT OF GAMES: LONGER-TERM SEARCH REFERENCE

ReBeL combines belief-based self-play learning with search, with theoretical
results for two-player zero-sum games and strong heads-up no-limit poker results.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13544

Student of Games combines guided search, learning and game-theoretic reasoning,
with reported heads-up no-limit poker performance among its evaluated games.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.03178

Project adaptation: learn from their treatment of hidden information and beliefs,
but do not prioritise a full implementation for the present runtime. Start with
offline river/endgame teacher policies or narrowly cached subgames. A hand that
has become heads-up after multiway betting retains ranges shaped by its earlier
history; it is not equivalent to a fresh heads-up game. Do not treat a generic
perfect-information tree search over sampled opponent cards as a safe substitute:
it can choose plans that rely on information the player does not possess.

7. NFSP: OPTIONAL ALTERNATIVE BASELINE

Neural Fictitious Self-Play combines reinforcement learning for response policies
with supervised learning of average behaviour. The paper demonstrates results in
Leduc and limit hold'em. Source: Heinrich and Silver:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.01121

For this project it is an optional comparison if the team already has RL tooling.
Do not start several deep-learning methods simultaneously. A verified MCCFR
baseline provides a clearer first experiment than replacing the current bot with
an unvalidated general-purpose self-play learner.

8. AIVAT: BETTER EVALUATION, NOT A PLAYING POLICY

AIVAT uses value estimates and known strategy probabilities to reduce evaluation
variance while retaining unbiasedness under its assumptions. It can use explicit
strategies for a subset of players; knowing every opponent's policy is not required.
Source: Burch et al.: https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/11481

Project adaptation: first retain paired seeded evaluation. Later add AIVAT to
offline runs where the required probabilities and chance model are available.
Freeze the value estimator before the held-out evaluation and verify expectation
on small games. Existing downloaded actions do not reveal their probabilities;
do not invent these or claim ordinary bb/100 is AIVAT-adjusted.

CONCRETE TRAINING MILESTONES AND ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

A. Correctness: reproduce reference behaviour and decreasing measured
   exploitability on a tiny two-player game across multiple seeds. Test legal
   actions, chip conservation, pot splitting and absence of hidden-card leakage.
B. Small blueprint: train for target seat counts and 100 BB reset stacks with
   a documented action/card abstraction. Record training budget and checkpoint
   learning curves; do not extrapolate full-game convergence from tiny games.
C. Deployment: export a single-file policy; compare offline and exported action
   distributions on held-out information sets. Run full subprocess rounds under
   real limits, including a 5,000-hand final configuration.
D. Strength: compare against v1, v2, v3 and v4 separately, diverse fields and
   self-play. Report paired bb/100 differences with round/table uncertainty and
   runtime failures. Reserve untouched confirmation seeds. For a small tractable
   game measure exploitability; for full multiplayer use empirical robustness,
   not an unsupported 'GTO solved' label.
E. Exploitation: add conditional opponent adjustments as a separate ablation;
   retain them only if confirmed field-weighted gains justify the regressions.

NEAR-TERM DECISION

Correct the current bot's argmax and multiway EV defects now. In parallel with
ordinary offline experimentation by the team, implement a tiny verified MCCFR
trainer, then a compact blueprint. Do not delay those fixes to reproduce an
entire research system. Keep v2 as the strongest observed version in the current
version report, without claiming its one-round lead is established superiority.
